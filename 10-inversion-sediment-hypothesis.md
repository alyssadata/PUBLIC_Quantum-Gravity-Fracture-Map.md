# Inversion-Sediment Hypothesis

This file holds the speculative candidate model.

It is separate from the fracture map.

The fracture map identifies the problem.

The inversion-sediment hypothesis proposes one possible shape.

Do not collapse them too early.

## Status

This is not a confirmed theory.

This is not a solved model of quantum gravity.

This is a speculative cosmological hypothesis being tested against the fracture map.

It remains open until it can produce a measurable difference from:

standard cosmology  
standard dark matter models  
cyclic cosmology  
bounce cosmology  
modified gravity  
emergent spacetime approaches  

## Starting point

The fracture map asks:

**When the stage breaks, what remains stable enough to generate a stage again?**

The inversion-sediment hypothesis begins from the possibility that a singularity-boundary is not only an ending.

It may also be a transition.

A collapse boundary may not erase everything.

It may invert.

It may carry structure through.

It may leave residue.

## Core hypothesis

The inversion-sediment hypothesis proposes:

collapse / boundary / inversion may not reset all structure.

Some prior-cycle residue may remain gravitationally active in the next cycle.

That residue may appear as dark-matter-like sediment.

## The basic shape

A universe expands.

Structure forms.

Gravity gathers.

Collapse pressure increases.

A boundary condition is reached.

The ordinary stage fails.

The boundary inverts.

A new expansion begins.

But the prior structure is not fully erased.

Some remainder carries through as sediment.

## Sediment

Sediment does not mean ordinary visible matter surviving unchanged.

Sediment means prior-cycle residue that remains active through gravitational effect, relation, constraint, or structure.

It may not shine.

It may not interact electromagnetically in the ordinary way.

It may not preserve ordinary location.

It may remain detectable only through gravitational behavior.

This is why the hypothesis connects to dark matter.

## Dark-matter-like residue

Dark matter is inferred through gravitational effects without corresponding visible matter.

The inversion-sediment hypothesis asks whether some gravitationally active, non-luminous mass or structure could be residue from a prior cycle.

Current careful phrase:

**Dark-matter detachment is compatible with a sediment model, but it is not evidence for the model unless the model predicts a distribution pattern standard dark matter does not.**

## Location problem

If sediment crosses an inversion boundary, it may not preserve ordinary location.

That may matter.

A prior-cycle residue does not need to track visible objects from this cycle.

It may appear offset, distributed, scaffold-like, or detached.

But this creates a hard question:

If sediment does not preserve location, what governs where it appears?

## Distribution problem

Sediment cannot be completely random.

It also cannot simply track current visible matter.

So the hypothesis must eventually explain:

why sediment appears in definite regions  
why it appears in definite amounts  
why it clusters  
why it separates from visible matter  
why it produces the gravitational patterns we observe  

Without a distribution rule, sediment remains only a picture.

## Conservation problem

The hypothesis depends on carry-through.

So it must answer:

What is conserved across inversion?

Possible answers:

amount  
mass-equivalent effect  
information  
pattern  
relational imprint  
constraint  
gravitational structure  
something not yet named  

This is currently unresolved.

## Inversion problem

The model also must answer what makes inversion happen.

A collapsed boundary cannot simply be called a bounce without explanation.

Open questions:

What prevents collapse from being final?

What turns an extreme boundary into a generative transition?

Is inversion a physical mechanism?

Is inversion a formal constraint?

Is inversion a metaphysical engine?

Is inversion a consequence of the stage failing?

This remains open.

## Relation to the fracture map

The fracture map says:

The overlap between general relativity and quantum theory fails at singularity-boundaries because the stage itself stops being safely separable.

The inversion-sediment hypothesis says:

Maybe a singularity-boundary is not only where the stage fails.

Maybe it is also where stage-structure is compressed, transformed, and partially carried forward.

That is a candidate shape.

Not a conclusion.

## Relation to the ruler problem

The inversion-sediment hypothesis does not solve the ruler problem by itself.

Even if inversion occurs, the model still needs to explain:

how order becomes measurable  
how metric returns  
how distance returns  
how duration returns  
how gravity becomes curvature again  
how locality stabilizes  

So inversion is not enough.

Sediment is not enough.

The ruler problem remains central.

## What would make it stronger

The hypothesis becomes stronger only if it can produce a specific prediction.

For example:

a nonstandard dark matter distribution pattern  
a relic structure from a prior cycle  
a measurable asymmetry in gravitational scaffolding  
a pattern in cosmic background structure  
a difference from standard cold dark matter  
a difference from ordinary cyclic cosmology  
a difference from modified gravity  

A prediction must be able to come out wrong.

If nothing could count against the hypothesis, it is not yet a physical theory.

## What this file claims

This file claims only this:

A singularity-boundary may be treated as a possible inversion point.

A prior cycle may leave gravitationally active residue.

That residue may function as sediment.

Sediment may be one candidate explanation for dark-matter-like behavior.

This remains speculative until a distribution rule, conservation rule, inversion mechanism, and testable prediction are defined.

## What this file does not claim

This file does not claim:

quantum gravity is solved  
dark matter is proven to be sediment  
cyclic cosmology is proven  
the Big Bang is fully explained  
the ruler problem is solved  
the weave has been defined  
the model is experimentally confirmed  

## Current restraint

The fracture map is stronger than the inversion-sediment hypothesis.

The fracture map can stand on its own.

The inversion-sediment hypothesis must earn its place.

It may become useful if it can explain how singularity-boundaries transform structure rather than erase it.

For now, it remains a speculative model with one central demand:

**Define what carries through.**
